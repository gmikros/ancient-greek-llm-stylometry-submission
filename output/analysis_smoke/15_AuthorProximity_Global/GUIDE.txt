ANALYSIS 15: AUTHOR PROXIMITY (GLOBAL)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

WHAT THIS SHOWS:
- How far each author's AI rewrites are from their human originals
- Which authors' styles are best/worst preserved by AI overall

VARIABLES INVOLVED:
- Category2 (Author): 10 Ancient Greek authors
- Category4 (Label): Human vs AI
- All numeric features

CALCULATION:
For each author:
1. Compute centroid (mean) of all Human originals
2. For each AI rewrite, compute Canberra distance to centroid
3. Average all distances for this author

DISTANCE METRICS EXPLAINED:

Canberra Distance:
- Weighted Manhattan distance
- Sensitive to small values (good for rare features)
- Handles features at different scales
- Traditional choice for stylometry

Cosine Distance (on Stylometric Features):
- **NOTE**: This is NOT Burrows' Delta/Cosine Delta (which uses word frequencies)
- Measures angular difference between stylometric feature vectors
- Range: 0 (identical direction) to 2 (opposite)
- Ignores magnitude, focuses on pattern
- Computed on your extracted features (mood, case, particle, etc.)

Euclidean Distance:
- Straight-line distance in feature space
- Sensitive to scale differences
- Standard geometric distance

Manhattan Distance (Cityblock):
- Sum of absolute differences
- Less sensitive to outliers than Euclidean
- Also called L1 distance

IMPORTANT CLARIFICATION:

The "Cosine Distance" metric here is computed on STYLOMETRIC FEATURES 
(mood percentages, case distributions, etc.), NOT on word frequencies.

This is DIFFERENT from:
- Burrows' Delta (uses word frequencies)
- Cosine Delta (authorship attribution standard on word frequencies)

Our cosine distance measures:
→ Angular difference between feature vectors
→ Whether AI preserves the PATTERN of features
→ Valid geometric distance, but not the classical "Delta" measure

For true Burrows'/Cosine Delta, you would need:
→ Raw text access
→ Word frequency extraction
→ Top 100-5000 most frequent words
→ Delta calculation on word distributions
